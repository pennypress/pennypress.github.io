# How it was made

The book was written as a **crawl**: a way of co-writing with an AI that is built around one rule.

> **The AI augments you. It does not replace you.** The person makes every creative decision.

The cheap failure of AI co-writing is a pleasant, competent, generic book that nobody in particular wrote. Everything below exists to prevent that. The method is called the *Writers' Crawl*, because it borrows the shape of a dungeon crawl: each stage of making a book is a floor, and every floor has a short ritual, a way to know you are done, and something you keep.

## The loop, in five moves

1. **The AI asks, one small cluster of questions at a time.** Gut answers are welcome. The author is never asked to justify a choice, because instinct is the signal and a justification turns it into homework.
2. **The AI offers two to four options, each with what it gives up.** The options must be really different. A lopsided menu is a decision the AI made in disguise.
3. **The author picks, merges, or rejects.**
4. **The author's exact words are saved**, typos and all, as the source of truth. Anything the AI adds is labelled as the AI's gloss, so by month two nobody confuses what the author said with what the AI inferred.
5. **Rejected options are kept**, with what would flip the answer. The AI may not bring one back in new clothes without asking.

At the end of every session the AI updates a **save file**: where we are, what is still undecided, what comes next. A chat with no save file forgets everything. A project with one does not.

## The floors

| Floor | You come out with |
|---|---|
| 0. Character creation | A taste profile: what you love, and what bores you |
| 1. Palette | World rules as yes/no dials, plus a never-list |
| 2. Premise and spine | Your pitch in your words, and the book's central question |
| 3. Cast | Leads with a flawed theory of themselves; a cast registry |
| 4. Beat sheet | The shape of the book and of every chapter |
| 5. Ledgers | Promises made to the reader, running gags (with retirement dates), influences |
| 6. Drafting | Chapters, one session loop at a time |
| 7. Revision | A better draft, and a list of what you chose to keep |
| 8. Spinoff portal | Side characters graduate; a second story in the same world |

You can stop at floor 7 and have a standalone novel. Floors are a default order, not a cage.

The method keeps a deadpan, kind announcer called the System, in the spirit of the announcer in *Dungeon Crawler Carl* but without the cruelty. It teases bureaucracy and never people, and it goes quiet and plain whenever a moment is about something real. Fun is load-bearing here, but it never replaces the true thing.

## The dials

The author can turn standing preferences up and down, and the AI follows them when it drafts. Each is a number from 0 to 10:

- **Humour against gravity**: comedy against seriousness (and the AI goes plain at grief whatever the dial says).
- **Pace**: linger and describe, or short scenes and hard chapter endings.
- **Action density**: how much physical movement and danger per chapter.
- **Philosophy density**: how much the characters wrestle with the book's big questions, openly.
- **Romance warmth**: how much tenderness is in the foreground, always age-appropriate.
- **Universe glimpses**: how often the story shows the larger world.
- **History visibility**: how much old history and myth shows through the surface.
- **Chapter length**: a target word count.

Every change to a dial is logged. A dial never overrides something the author says out loud in a session.

## The ledger of choices

Some decisions reach past the page they were made on: a rule about the world, a thing that must never appear, a line we will not cross. Those go in one file, the **choices ledger**, one entry each, newest last. An entry looks like this (this one is invented, for illustration):

```
## C-007: the ferry only runs at night
- date: 2026-01-14
- status: decided
- choice: The river ferry runs only after dark, so every river scene is a night scene.
- words: > "the ferry should be a secret, nobody sees it in daylight"
- rejected: A, a daytime ferry (too ordinary; flips if the plot needs crowds).
- scope: book-only
- touches: ch03, ch09; the ferryman's note in the world bible
- reversible: costly (it reopens two chapters)
- landed: not yet written
- source: the planning notes where it was discussed
```

An entry with no source is a defect. The ledger answers one question quickly: *what has the author decided, what does it touch, and could we take it back?* It matters most when you start a sequel or let someone else write in your world.

For this book, the ledger currently holds **6 entries**.

## What the AI is for

In this method the AI carries the heavy volume: research, continuity, ledgers, recaps, and drafts when asked. It keeps the book consistent across 157,000 words. It does not choose what the book is about, what it risks, or what it means. That stays with the person.
